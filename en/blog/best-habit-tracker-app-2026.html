Comparison

What is the best habit tracker app in 2026?

I've used, tested, or studied each of these apps in depth. This is an honest comparison, with clear criteria: privacy, simplicity, design, and most importantly, what actually helps you build habits that last.

IB
Italo Bandeira Software Engineer
Aug 01, 2026 9 min read

Transparency notice: I'm the creator of BetterDay. I'm going to speak well of it, because that's what I believe after years of using other apps and building my own. But I'll also speak honestly about its downsides, where it makes sense. And I'll show why it's the best choice for most people, while acknowledging that other tools shine in specific cases.

The criteria

Before the comparison, the filters I used:

  • Privacy: where does your data live?
  • Simplicity: can you open the app and check off a habit in 3 taps or less?
  • Progress visualization: can you see the picture of months of habit at a glance?
  • Real cost: price, subscription, and what's behind the paywall.
  • Real adherence: does the gamification help or hurt over time?

BetterDay (Android)

Pros: 100% of the data stays on the device, no server. Total privacy guaranteed by design. Not a promise, an architecture. Clean interface, single-tap tracking, a colorful grid that shows your full history at a glance. Motivating streaks without a competitive leaderboard. Daily notes per habit. Statistics that surface patterns (your best days, longest streak, etc.). Fully free, no ads, no subscription, no paywall. Reminder per habit.

Cons: Android-only for now (iOS in development). No multi-device sync, because the data never leaves the device. No RPG-style gamification (which can be a plus or minus depending on your profile).

Who it's for: anyone who wants simplicity, privacy, and an app that respects the user's time. People who've already tried more complex ones and given up.

Habitica (Android, iOS, Web)

The oldest habit app on the list. Heavy gamification: you have an avatar, earn XP, gold, and fight monsters. Every habit completed is an attack; a failed habit costs HP. The look mixes pixel art and classic RPG.

Pros: the gamification is addictive for RPG fans. Active community, group challenges, guilds. Available on all platforms with cloud sync. Open source.

Cons: data lives on a server (you need an account). The interface is dense and dated. Designers complain, regular users accept it. The gamification can become the end in itself: you do the habit for the XP, not for the habit. Paying premium unlocks pets, quests, and customization.

Who it's for: anyone who loves RPGs and needs an extra layer of extrinsic engagement. People who get distracted easily and respond well to variable rewards.

Streaks (iOS)

One of the most beautiful apps out there for iPhone. Apple-Approved design, with deep system integration: you can check off a habit right from the iPhone's home screen without opening the app.

Pros: impeccable look, deep iOS integration (Siri, widgets, Watch). Smart notifications that adapt to the time you usually complete. Reasonable privacy. Data stays in iCloud if you enable it.

Cons: iOS-only. Limited to 24 habits (or 12 in the free version). No notes, no advanced stats, no monthly progress view. More expensive: the annual subscription runs around R$ 30-40 (about $6-8 USD, Brazil pricing).

Who it's for: iOS users who value native integration and aesthetics over feature depth.

Productive (Android, iOS)

A middle ground. More polished than Habitica, more complex than BetterDay. Brings ready-made habit suggestions, "routine" sessions, and app blocking during a habit.

Pros: solid design, library of ready habits, "routine" mode to sequence habits. Available on both platforms. Friend challenges.

Cons: heavy subscription (R$ 50-80/year, depending on plan, roughly $10-16 USD). Important features behind the paywall. Data lives on a server (requires account). The interface tends to feel "too much" for those who want simplicity.

Who it's for: anyone who wants a more "complete" app and is willing to pay.

Fabulous (Android, iOS)

The philosophical opposite of BetterDay. It's not just a habit app. It's a full routine coach, with journeys, rituals, coaching, and a strong gamification layer. Backed by Duke University research on habit formation.

Pros: pretty, guided journey for new users, embedded behavioral science content, iOS/Android health integration.

Cons: the most intrusive app on the list. Constant notifications, insistent "coaching," pushes premium content aggressively. Data on a server. The free version is very limited. Annual subscription around R$ 100 (about $20 USD). Has a questionable privacy track record (ads, data sharing with partners).

Who it's for: total beginners who need a firm hand holding them through. Not for those who want autonomy.

The direct comparison

To summarize, across 5 criteria (1 = bad, 5 = great):

  • Privacy: BetterDay 5, Streaks 4, Habitica 3, Productive 3, Fabulous 2.
  • Simplicity: BetterDay 5, Streaks 4, Productive 3, Fabulous 3, Habitica 2.
  • Progress visualization: BetterDay 5, Streaks 4, Habitica 3, Productive 4, Fabulous 3.
  • Cost-benefit: BetterDay 5 (totally free), Habitica 4 (generous free), Streaks 3, Productive 2, Fabulous 1.
  • Long-term adherence: BetterDay 5, Streaks 4, Habitica 4 (for RPG fans), Productive 3, Fabulous 3.

Why BetterDay wins for most people

The key point isn't "who has more features." It's "who respects the user enough not to clutter the interface with mechanics that distract from what matters." To build a habit, you need three things:

  1. Check off what you did (fast, easy, friction-free).
  2. See the progress (visual, immediate, rewarding).
  3. Feel in control (no pressure, no leaderboard, no subscription).

BetterDay does all three. Most of the other apps try to solve the problem by adding mechanics. Which, ironically, is the kind of thing that sabotages the habit. You end up managing the app instead of managing your life.

Don't believe it? Download BetterDay, create 3 habits, and try it for a week. Come back here and tell me if the colorful grid, the daily note, and the streak were worth more than XP, mascots, and insistent notifications.

When to choose something else

It would be dishonest not to acknowledge:

  • If you love RPGs and need game mechanics to stay engaged: Habitica.
  • If you're 100% on iOS and want native integration: Streaks.
  • If you want guided coaching and don't mind a heavy subscription: Fabulous.
  • If you want a library of routines and social challenges: Productive.

But if you want an app that does the basics really well, respects your data, and doesn't try to hook you on the app instead of helping you live: BetterDay.
